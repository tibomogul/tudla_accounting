# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- A `tax_registration` setting for what printed documents say about tax. `TaxRegistration.australia(abn)` heads an invoice "Tax invoice", prints your ABN, calls the tax GST, and warns about an ABN that's missing or fails its check digits. The README lists what we couldn't confirm with the ATO.
- The BAS template adds W4, W3, W5 and the summary labels 4, 5A, 5B, 8A, 8B and 9, and G1's name says to answer Yes to the form's GST question. Forms already set up don't change. The README lists what we couldn't confirm with the ATO. The demo's BAS now shows exports, GST-free sales, capital purchases and tax withheld from wages.
- Withholding codes have `withheld_on`: `approval` (a bill line's, as before) or `payment`, for tax withheld from a supplier when they're paid. A payment to a supplier can then list `withholdings` (`Payment.record!`, the API, and the payment form, which shows them once there's such a code and, paying a bill of a supplier whose default code it is, starts with the tax on what's owed), the tax worked out from the amount it's withheld from. Pay runs withhold it from a supplier whose default code it is, and recording a bank line as paying such a supplier's bill takes the line as the cash after the tax. A starter list adds Australia's no-ABN withholding at 47%. Run the new migration.
- Taxable payments: a contact can be marked `taxable_payments` (on its page, the contacts import and export, and the API), and a new report, Taxable payments, by payee, lists what was paid to each in a run of months, the last financial year by default, with the GST in it and any tax withheld, on screen, in CSV and Excel, and in the API (`taxable_payments`). It's for Australia's TPAR; the README lists what we couldn't confirm with the ATO. Run the new migration.
- The demo's Demo Co has a supplier with no ABN, paid with 47% withheld under `NOABN`, so its BAS shows W4 and the payee list and payment summaries have last year's payments.
- A new report, Payments with tax withheld, by payee, lists each supplier's gross payments and the tax withheld under codes withheld on payment, for the last financial year by default, on screen, in CSV and Excel, and in the API (`payee_payments`). It's for the year-end report of no-ABN withholding, and flags a payee with no address. Each payee's payment summary downloads as a PDF, one or all of them, listing its payments, to sign and give them; it's the payer's own statement, not a copy of an ATO form.
- A tax return box can add what was credited to an account (`account_credits`), less reversals of those credits, so a payment to the tax office doesn't reduce an amount withheld. Run the new migration.

### Changed

- The README gathers what the BIR hasn't confirmed for Philippine filings into one list, and adds that putting capital goods on the 2550Q's item 44A is our reading of the form.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-05

Tagged but not published to RubyGems.

### Added

- A purchases tax code can say what its lines are bought as: services, capital goods or other goods (`purchase_type`, "Bought as" on its form, and in `GET /tax_codes`). It starts unset.
- Summary lists of sales and purchases, for the Philippines' SLSP: a row per customer or supplier per month, on screen, in CSV and Excel, and in the API (`sales_list`, `purchases_list`). The downloads have columns for final VAT withheld and non-creditable input tax, at zero; the pages leave them out. A line goes by its tax code; one with no code counts as exempt, with the amount flagged. Each list shows any difference from the tax summary, and flags a missing TIN or a purchases code with no type. They go by posting date, with a notice on cash-basis books. There's no DAT file or importation list. The fields follow RR 16-2005, but we couldn't find the BIR's record layout, so the column order isn't confirmed.
- The demo's Demo Pilipinas Inc. buys capital goods, on a purchases code of their own, so its purchases list splits them from the services. Its 2550Q includes that code under domestic purchases, and its figures match the summary lists.

### Fixed

- The 2307 page's missing TIN message no longer ends in two full stops when the last supplier's name ends in one.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-04

Tagged but not published to RubyGems.

### Added

- Withholding codes (`WithholdingCode`): a tax withheld from payments, with its rate and the accounts it posts to, and a contact's default. `WithholdingCodeTemplates` has a starter list of the Philippines' common expanded withholding codes; check its rates before relying on them. `GET /withholding_codes` lists them in the API.
- Withholding from suppliers on bills: a bill or supplier credit's line can have a withholding code, starting from the contact's default. Approving posts the tax to the code's account, so the payable is the amount left to pay, and keeps a `WithheldTax` row for each line; voiding adds an opposite row. The bill, its PDF and the payment notice show what was withheld. The base is the line's amount before VAT, which commentary supports but we haven't confirmed in a BIR issuance. A code's rate and accounts can't change once tax is posted under it.
- Tax customers withhold from payments: a payment received can list what they withheld, as their certificate gives it. The tax posts to the code's creditable account and the receivable is credited with the cash and the tax, so the invoice is settled; only the cash can be refunded. The receipt shows it. On a payment started from an invoice, the cash comes down by the tax as it's typed, until the cash is changed by hand. A new Tax certificates page, from Payments received, lists the amounts by customer and quarter, for chasing certificates and marking them received, one at a time or a customer's whole quarter. A payment can't be voided while its certificate is marked. In the API, `POST /payments` takes `withholdings`, and `POST` and `DELETE /withheld_taxes/:id/certificate` mark and unmark. The payments CSV gains a last column, "Tax withheld".
- Withholding reports: a summary by code and month, the tax withheld from suppliers (QAP) and from you (SAWT, certificates received only), on screen, in CSV and Excel, and in the API (`withholding`, `qap`, `sawt`). Each shows any difference from the accounts' movement, and flags a contact with no TIN. Their columns come from secondary sources, not the BIR's own specification, and there's no DAT file yet.
- Certificates to suppliers: a new report lists a BIR Form 2307 for each supplier tax was withheld from in any three months in a row (the current filing quarter by default, with the filing quarters listed first), and makes the PDF for one supplier or all of them, on the form's 8.5 x 13 inch paper, following the first page of the January 2018 edition, without its seal and barcode. A new `withholding_agent` setting gives your TIN, address, ZIP code and signatory. Signatures and the business tax lines stay blank, and names and addresses stop at two lines. Whether the BIR accepts a rebuilt form isn't confirmed: check with your RDO.
- The demo's Demo Pilipinas Inc. withholds tax: the starter codes, a consultant billed at 10%, a contractor at 2%, and a customer withholding 2%. Existing demo books don't get these until they're rebuilt with `bin/setup --reset`.
- Contacts have a postal code, apart from the address: on their form and page, under the address on invoices, bills and payment notices, in the API (`postal_code`), and in imports and the contacts CSV, which gains a last column, "Postal code". It fills the ZIP code box on BIR Form 2307.

### Fixed

- A long confirmation on a form's submit button, such as voiding a payment, ran out of its card. It now wraps inside it.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-03

Tagged but not published to RubyGems.

### Changed
- The statements page and emailing every statement (`PartyStatement.to_send`) read all the customers' or suppliers' items and age them in one go, in the same number of queries however many there are, rather than about 20 queries for each.
- **Breaking:** the pages read typed numbers (amounts, quantities, exchange rates, tax and budget percentages) in the organization's number format, and fill their fields in it, so books whose currency writes `1.234,56` (the euro, say) now type `1234,50` rather than `1234.50`, which is refused, saying how to write it. Set `number_format = :point` to keep reading `1234.50` there. The JSON API, CSV and Excel files and imports are unchanged.
- `BalanceRebuilder#own_amounts` lists accounts with sub-accounts holding posted lines or an opening balance of their own, from books posted before that was refused, and `tudla_accounting:balances:check` warns about each (without failing). `OwnPostingsMover.call(account, to: sub_account)` moves such an account's own posted lines onto one of its sub-accounts, keeping its balances, rebuilding the sub-account's and recording `account.postings_moved`; lines in closed periods are refused.
- The forms offer only accounts without sub-accounts (`Account.postable`) wherever a line will post: entries, invoice and bill lines, recurring entries and invoices, payments and the pay run, bank rules, the banking page, a contact's default account and a tax code's account. A record that already names one with sub-accounts keeps it, marked "(has sub-accounts)". `Entry.create_from_ruby_hash` (and so the JSON API), `BankReconciler#create_entry!` and invoice and bill lines refuse such an account straight away, with the same message as posting, where they failed only on posting or approval.
- **Breaking:** only an account without sub-accounts takes postings, so a parent account always holds what its sub-accounts add up to, in every period. A line on an account with sub-accounts is refused when saved (naming them), and posting one written before its account took sub-accounts raises `ArgumentError`; a sub-account is refused beneath an account with posted lines or an opening balance of its own, or holding a role other than receivables or payables, and those roles go only to accounts without sub-accounts. On PostgreSQL, triggers refuse posting to an account with sub-accounts and sub-accounts beneath one with posted lines from anywhere (a new migration installs them). `Account#posting_blocker` and `#sub_account_blocker` say why. `USAGE.md` adds a 6030 Bank fees account, which its bank rule posts to.
- **Breaking:** the receivable and payable accounts are the totals of a sub-account for each currency. Giving either role makes the one in the organization's currency (`1100-USD`), moving the account's own opening balance onto it (the role is refused when that code is taken elsewhere), and invoices, bills, payments and imported open items in that currency post there rather than to the account itself; importing a chart with the role puts the account's opening balance there too. A foreign-currency sub-account (`1100-EUR`) is no longer made beneath a receivable or payable account with postings of its own, which would then hold more than its sub-accounts add up to: `Account.for_role_in_currency!` raises `ArgumentError` instead. A host's own entry sources should post to `Account.for_role_in_currency!` rather than the account holding the role.
- The Sales and Purchases lists, the pay run, the payment form, a contact's page and the reminders due no longer read each document's organization one at a time (`Document.with_open_cents` preloads it), and the lists add up what is owed in one query (`Document.open_base_cents_net`) rather than loading every approved document: with 2,500 invoices and bills, Sales went from 1,792 queries and 1.5 seconds to 11 and 0.08, and the pay run from 735 to 18.
- The receivables and payables aging, the dashboard and statements read what is owed on every open invoice, bill and payment in a few queries, where they took two for each: with 2,500 invoices and bills the aging went from 7,700 queries to 12, the dashboard from 14 seconds to 0.6, and the statements for every customer from 14 seconds to under 2. `CarryingAmount.with_outstanding` loads carrying amounts that way, and `Entry#posted_reversal` preloads entries' reversals.
- Excel downloads give each amount its currency's decimal places (`#,##0` for the yen, `#,##0.000` for the Bahraini dinar), where every amount had two.
- Amounts are written one way (`MoneyFormatter`), in the organization's style whatever their currency. Headings, messages, errors, emails and the audit trail give the organization's currency symbol (`$1,234.56`) and any other currency's code (`USD 1,234.56`), where they gave each currency's own symbol and marks (`$` for the US and Australian dollar alike, `€1.234,56` in Australian books); a negative is `-$5.00`. Some that gave a bare number (`Applied 120.00`, `Out by 2.00`, a pay run's `AUD 300.00`) now give the symbol. PDFs now write foreign amounts in the organization's style, as the pages do, and the forms' live totals use its separators (`1.500,00` for books in euros). Views have `tc_money_text` for an amount standing alone.
- **Breaking:** `Entry.create_from_ruby_hash` (and so `create_entry_from_source!`) reads a line's amount strictly: a currency code and a number (`"USD 1,100.00"`, `"USD -1100"`, `"USD (1100)"`), or a number alone, which is now in the organization's currency rather than the `base_currency` setting's. Anything else raises `ArgumentError` naming the line (`line 2: lots isn't an amount`), where it was read leniently: `"USD lots"` as zero, `"USD (5)"` as +5, `"USD 1e3"` as 13.00, a symbol (`"$1,100"`) in the base currency, and extra decimal places rounded. The `fx` amount and rate are read the same way. `AmountParser.text(money)` writes a `Money` as these read it; `Money#to_s` uses the currency's own decimal mark (`1100,00` for the euro).
- Amounts and other numbers typed in the pages or given to the JSON API are read one way (`AmountParser`): `.` for decimals, commas only as thousands separators, a sign or parentheses for a negative. An amount with more decimal places than its currency has (`10.005` dollars, `12.5` yen) is refused instead of rounded, and so are `1e3`, `1_000` and commas that don't group thousands (`10,00`); the error says why. `(5.00)` now reads as -5 where it was refused, and the API now takes thousands commas. Imports still round extra decimal places, now as the `rounding` setting says (a budget spreadsheet's number cells always rounded half up). The forms' live totals read amounts the same way.
- **Breaking:** the general ledger CSV has new columns: `Entry no.` and `Reference` after the date, and `Description` (the line's) after the entry.
- Entries are shown with their numbers across the pages (ledgers, banking, payments applied) and on the audit trail.
- **Breaking:** the accounts the books treat specially are marked by role on each organization's own accounts (`Account#role`: `receivable`, `payable`, `retained_earnings`, `unrealized_fx_gain`, `realized_fx_gain`), instead of named by code in the settings, so organizations' charts can differ (a bookkeeper's clients) and accounts can be renumbered. Set a role in the account form, with a `Role` column in the chart of accounts import, with `role:` in `AccountsCreator` (either refuses a role given to more than one of its accounts), or with `AccountRoleAssigner.save(account, role)`; `Account.for_role(organization, role)` finds it. Each role needs a suitable category. Roles change only through `AccountRoleAssigner` (`save`, or `save!` to raise), which the form, the import and `AccountsCreator` use; it moves a role from the account holding it, and a plain `account.update(role:)` is refused. In the form, only those with the `:administer` permission can change a role. Receivables and payables stay on an account once it, or an account beneath it, has posted lines (its open items would be left behind), and can't be given to such an account, whose lines opened nothing owed; either way, opening balances and drafts don't count.
- Marking the retained earnings account, moving it or taking it off rebuilds the organization's balances in the same transaction, so the earlier years' profit is carried where it now belongs (a client taken on mid-stream). `AccountRoleAssigner.batch(organization) { |assigner| ... }` makes several changes with one rebuild, all or nothing: if one is refused, none is made. Retained earnings can't change while a period after the first year is closed (reopen them first), so closed periods keep the balances they were closed with.
- Every rebuild's `balances.rebuilt` audit event now lists the accounts it corrected (`accounts`).
- Revaluation needs an unrealized gains account only when there's something to revalue.
- Errors about a missing account name the organization, through the new `organization_label` setting (default: class and id).
- `ForexRateRetriever` gives every rate to the 8 decimal places the rate columns hold, an inverse of a stored rate and a provider's crossed rate included, so the first lookup and later ones (read back from `ForexRate`) agree, as do revaluation and exchange gain figures with the rates documents and payments store. A provider rate too small to keep raises `RateNotFound`.

### Removed
- **Breaking:** the `receivable_account_code`, `payable_account_code`, `retained_earnings_account_code`, `unrealized_fx_gain_account_code` and `realized_fx_gain_account_code` settings: mark the accounts' roles instead (in your chart templates, `AccountsCreator` data and import files). Setting one raises `TudlaAccounting::ConfigurationError` saying which role to mark. There's no upgrade path from 0.2.0, which was never published.

### Added
- Books of accounts (`Reports::BooksOfAccounts`, Reports → Books of accounts): the general journal, general ledger, cash receipts, cash disbursements, sales and purchase journals, in numbered pages of 30 rows with page totals and checks, a page to a printed sheet, and as a PDF. They follow the BIR's loose-leaf books for the Philippines. Each entry goes in one book (by the cash accounts, then by invoice or bill), and the sales journal splits VATable, zero-rated and exempt sales by tax code treatment. The new `books_header` setting heads each page, and falls back to the `document_header`. With caxlsx, all six books download as one Excel workbook (`Reports::BooksWorkbook`): a cover with each book's page count, then a sheet a book in the templates' layout, a page break after each 30-row page, scaled to print a page to an A4 sheet. The demo sets `cash_account_codes` (Cash and bank, 1010), so Demo Pilipinas Inc. has all six books to try. **Sample format** (`Reports::SampleBooks`) makes a page of each book with made-up rows, for the application to use loose-leaf books; it needs no financial year.
- Tax codes have a `treatment`: `standard`, `zero_rated` or `exempt` (set on the tax code form, and in the API's `GET /tax_codes`), so codes without tax can be reported apart, as zero-rated and exempt sales are on a VAT return. Zero-rated and exempt codes have a rate of 0. Existing codes are `standard`.
- A tax return template for the Philippines' quarterly VAT return, BIR Form 2550Q (April 2024): `TaxReturnForm.from_template!(organization, "ph_2550q")` lays out Part IV's computation (items 31A to 61B) and Part II's total payable (items 15 to 26) with their totals, for mapping the VATable, zero-rated and exempt sales and the purchases to tax codes
- `BspForexRateProvider`: exchange rates from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas for books in pesos (its daily bulletin, peso–dollar and cross rates), and `BspRatesJob`, which stores each day's bulletin so every currency keeps its rate until the BSP's monthly cross rates catch up. What it reads is kept in `Rails.cache` (the cross rates for a day, the rest for an hour), so other processes and restarts don't download it again
- A `number_format` setting: `:point` (`1,234.56`) or `:comma` (`1.234,56`), or a callable choosing one per organization; nil (the default) keeps each organization's currency's own marks. Pages, PDFs, emails and messages write amounts in it (`NumberFormat.for(organization)`), and quantities, exchange rates and tax percentages too (`tc_number`; `TaxCode#rate_percent` and `#label`), as do the forms' live totals. `MoneyFormatter.number` and `.labelled` now take the organization (or, as before, a currency). `AmountParser.decimal`, `.cents` and `.text` take a `format:`, and `.number_text` writes other numbers.
- The supplier pay run in the JSON API: `GET /pay_run` lists the bills due that a bank account could pay, and `POST /pay_runs` (`post`) pays a batch of them, one payment per supplier, optionally emailing their payment notices, once per `Idempotency-Key`. `PayRun.call` takes an `idempotency_key`, keying each supplier's payment `"<key>:<supplier id>"`.
- Recurring invoices in the JSON API: `GET /recurring_invoices` (and `/:id`), set up and changed with `POST` and `PATCH` (`record`; `post` too to approve or email their invoices), `DELETE`, `POST /recurring_invoices/run` (`post`) to make the invoices due today, and `POST /recurring_invoices/:id/make` (`record`, with an `Idempotency-Key`) for a one-off draft.
- A contact's currency (`Contact#currency`, none for the organization's own): the one their new invoices, bills and credit notes start in, in the form (choosing the contact switches it) and the JSON API (unless one is given; a credit note takes its invoice's), and their new payments too when the books have a bank account in it. The contact form and page, the contacts CSV (a `Currency` column at the end), the import and the API (`currency`) have it.
- `perf/benchmark.rb` also builds contacts, invoices and bills (some in euros), payments, credit notes, refunds, a recurring invoice and a budget, and times the services and pages for them: statements, the pay run, reminders, recurring invoices, budgets and the Sales, Purchases, payments and contacts pages.
- `USAGE.md` adds a second part, running the books: invoices and bills (with one in another currency), payments, credit notes and refunds, recurring invoices, pay runs, statements and reminders, banking with bank rules, and budgets, each in the pages and from the console. Its examples run in the suite, from the console and in the browser.
- `USAGE.md` adds customers and suppliers as step 7 (importing contacts, or adding them one at a time), and the settings for sending documents, PDFs and attachments to step 1. Its examples run in the suite, as before.
- Journal numbers: posting gives an entry the next number of its series (`Entry#number`, shown by `number_label`), with no gaps, since drafts have none and posted entries are never deleted. The `entry_number_series` setting numbers entries in one run per organization (the default), per financial year, or by a callable; `entry_number_format` shows them (a format string or a callable; `#42` by default). Either can change later without renumbering what is posted. Entries posted before the upgrade are numbered in the order they were created.
- `Entry#reference` for a cheque number, a supplier's invoice number and the like: in the entry form, `create_from_ruby_hash(reference:)`, kept on reversals. The entries page searches references and numbers too.
- A description on each entry line (`Detail#description`): a field under each line's account in the entry form, `description:` on a line in `create_from_ruby_hash`, kept on reversals, shown on the entry and in the ledgers, and searched on the entries page. An entry made from a bank statement line under other particulars keeps the statement's description on the bank line.
- Attachments on entries, with Active Storage in the host app: `Entry#add_attachments!` attaches receipts and documents to drafts and posted entries, checked against the `attachment_content_types` and `attachment_max_size` settings, and `Entry#remove_attachment!` removes them (from a posted entry only with a reason). Both are on the audit trail with the file's checksum (`entry.attachment_added`, `entry.attachment_removed`). The entry page lists, uploads and removes them (a posted entry's only with `:administer`), serving files through the engine so its permissions apply; the entries list marks entries with files. The install generator says how to set up Active Storage.
- Inactive accounts: `Account#deactivate!` (the account page's Make inactive) stops offering an account and those beneath it for new entries, keeping their history, balances and reports; `reactivate!` offers one again. Refused for an account holding a role, taking an active tax code's tax, or with receivables or payables still open (`deactivation_blocker`). The entry form, banking forms and account choices leave inactive accounts out (filters mark them), and `Entry.create_from_ruby_hash` and `BankReconciler#create_entry!` refuse them. Recorded as `account.deactivated` and `account.reactivated`.
- Comparative statements: the balance sheet and profit and loss compare the period chosen with the previous one or the same one a year earlier (with the change and percentage change), or show each month of the year (with the year's total in profit and loss), through the pages' Compare with choice or `Reports::Comparison`.
- Reports download as Excel workbooks too (`.xlsx`, `Reports::Table#to_xlsx`) when the host app bundles the optional `caxlsx` gem (`TudlaAccounting.xlsx?`), and print cleanly (Print or save as PDF): no navigation or forms, light colours, every column of a wide table, landscape for wide comparisons.
- Every report downloads as CSV (each page's Download CSV link, or its path with `.csv`), as shown on the page, comparisons included; `Reports::Tables` lays each one out as a `Reports::Table`. Text a spreadsheet would run as a formula is written with a leading apostrophe, in the general ledger's CSV too.
- Bank rules: record unmatched statement lines by rules (description contains, direction, amount range, bank account → account, tax code, dimension values, particulars), suggested on the banking page, one line or all at once, and started from a line. `BankReconciler#create_entry!` takes `tax_code:` and `dimension_values:`.
- Budgets (`Budget`, `BudgetLine`): named plans for a year, an amount per income and expense account per month, one of them the year's main budget. Started blank, from a year's actual results changed by a percentage, or from another budget; entered on a grid that keeps totals and spreads a year's amount over the months; downloaded and uploaded as CSV or Excel. On the audit trail as `budget.created`, `budget.updated` and `budget.deleted`.
- Tax returns (`TaxReturnForm`, `TaxReturnBox`, `TaxReturnBoxPart`, `TaxReturnCalculator`): forms of boxes, each adding or subtracting tax codes' net amounts, tax or gross amounts, accounts' movement and earlier boxes, optionally in whole units; templates for Australia's BAS and the UK VAT return; set up under Setup → Tax returns and shown, box by box with their parts, on the Tax return report (CSV and Excel too). Tax codes and accounts a return uses can't be deleted.
- Refunds: paying back what is left of a customer's payment or credit note, or recording a supplier's refund of theirs, as a payment of kind `refund` or `supplier_refund` (`Payment.record!(refund_of:)`), from the payment's, credit note's or contact's page, or through the API (`POST /payments` with `refund_of`). Refunds are listed with the payments (the payments CSV has a `Kind` column at the end), and a receipt lists what was refunded of it.
- Applying a leftover credit from the payment's or credit note's page and the contact's page, not only the entry's, and through the API (`POST /payments/:id/apply`, `POST /documents/:id/apply`).
- Dimensions on invoice and bill lines: a column per dimension in the form, `dimensions` on each line in the API, shown on the document's page (not on the printed document). Approving it tags each line's posting, so profit and loss by dimension includes sales and purchases; a credit note copies its lines' values from what it credits.
- Files on invoices, bills and credit notes (the supplier's PDF on a bill, say), as on entries, on their pages and through the API (`attachments` in an entry's or document's JSON; `POST` and `GET .../attachments`). Attaching and removing files is shared by entries and documents (`Attachable`), and is on the audit trail as `document.attachment_added` and `document.attachment_removed` too.
- Supplier pay runs (`PayRun`): Purchases → Pay run picks the bills owed by a date from one bank account's currency, for all suppliers or one, and pays them in one go, one payment per supplier, all or nothing, with each supplier's payment notice emailed.
- Payments in other currencies: a payment, refund or supplier's refund through a bank account held in another currency is in that currency at `Payment#exchange_rate` (looked up when not given), posted converted with its foreign amount on the bank and receivable or payable lines, and applied to documents in that currency, posting the realized exchange gain or loss. The payment form asks for the currency, and offers its accounts and what is owed in it; receipts, notices, lists, the CSV (a `Currency` column at the end) and the API (`currency`, `exchange_rate`; amounts in the account's currency) show it in its currency. Receivables and payables, and their sub-accounts, can't be chosen as the bank account.
- Invoices, bills and credit notes in other currencies (`Document#currency`, `#exchange_rate`, looked up for the date when not given): posted converted at the rate, the total to the receivable or payable account's sub-account in that currency (made the first time) with its foreign amount; shown, printed, emailed and listed in their currency (the totals in the organization's), and in the API (`currency`, `exchange_rate`, `amount_due_in_base`). The documents CSV has `Currency` and `Due now` in the organization's currency at the end. They take payments, refunds and credits only in their currency.
- Recurring invoices (`RecurringInvoice`, `RecurringInvoiceRunner`, `RecurringInvoicesJob`): a customer's invoice made on a schedule, as a draft, approved, or approved and emailed, as each template says, never twice for a date; its lines' dimension values carry over. Under Sales → Recurring invoices, started blank or from an invoice's **Repeat**, with problems shown on the template and the dashboard. On the audit trail as `recurring_invoice.created`, `.updated`, `.deleted` and `.generated`. Recurring entries share the schedule (`RecurringSchedule`).
- Overdue reminders: with `reminder_days` set, customers of overdue invoices are emailed a reminder on those days after the due date, each once, from Sales → Reminders due or by `SendRemindersJob` (which the host schedules), using `ReminderRunner`; and any overdue invoice can be reminded by hand from its page.
- Emailing statements of account (one, or every one the statements page shows, naming those with no email address) and payments' receipts and payment notices (from the payment's page, or as the payment is recorded), as invoices are emailed. A contact's page lists their statements' emails.
- Recording a payment with nothing to apply it to (no open invoices or bills) no longer fails.
- Something not found asked for as a PDF or CSV (another organization's invoice, say) answers 404 instead of failing.
- Emailing invoices and credit notes from their pages (`Delivery`, `DeliveryJob`, `DocumentMailer`, `EmailDraft`): the email starts from a template (`document_email_templates`) to the contact, can be changed, and is sent in the background through the host's ActionMailer setup with the PDF attached, from `document_mail_from` and replying to `document_mail_reply_to`; or through the host's own mailer (`document_mailer`). Each email is recorded as queued, sent or failed (with the error, to send again), listed on the document's page, marked on the Sales list, and on the audit trail as `email.sent` and `email.failed`.
- The engine's `ApplicationMailer` no longer sets a From of its own (it was the generator's placeholder): the host's ActionMailer default applies.
- PDFs of invoices, bills and credit notes, statements of account, and payments (a receipt, or a payment notice), made in headless Chrome from the layouts the pages print when the host bundles the optional `ferrum` gem (`TudlaAccounting.pdf?`, `PdfMaker`, Download PDF on their pages). Settings: `pdf_browser_options`, `pdf_paper`, `pdf_stylesheet`. Statements are headed by `document_header` too, and a payment's page shows its receipt or payment notice, ready to print.
- A JSON API at `api/v1` under the engine's path (see `API.md`), reached with an API token. Reading (`read`): the organization, accounts, entries, contacts, invoices and bills, payments, tax codes, periods and the reports, paged, with filters and `updated_since` for syncing. Recording: entries, contacts, and invoices and bills as drafts (`record`); posting and reversing entries, approving and voiding invoices and bills, recording and voiding payments (`post`). Creates take an `Idempotency-Key` header; documents and payments keep it (`idempotency_key`), as entries do. Each token may make `api_rate_limit` requests a minute (300 by default), counted in `api_rate_limit_store` (`Rails.cache` by default).
- API tokens (`ApiToken`, Setup → API tokens): issued with permissions and an optional expiry, the secret shown once and kept only as a digest, last use noted, revoked rather than deleted; a token acts as itself on the audit trail. On the audit trail as `api_token.created` and `api_token.revoked`.
- Payments (`Payment`, Record payment, the Payments received and Payments made pages): money received or paid through a bank or cash account, posted straight away and applied to the contact's invoices or bills as chosen, the rest kept as a credit; voided by reversing their entry. A bank statement line can be recorded as a payment of an open invoice or bill, and is matched to it. On the audit trail as `payment.recorded` and `payment.voided`.
- Invoices, bills and credit notes (`Document`, the Sales and Purchases pages): drafted line by line with quantities, prices, accounts and tax codes, approved (numbered per kind with `document_number_format`, and posted, opening the contact's receivable or payable, or a credit applied to the document it credits), voided by reversing their entry, and printed or saved as PDF headed by `document_header`. Listed by status with what is still owed, and downloaded as CSV. On the audit trail as `document.created`, `.updated`, `.deleted`, `.approved` and `.voided`.
- Contacts (`Contact`, the Contacts page): customers and suppliers with their details, payment terms and default account and tax code, each showing what they owe or are owed, searched, downloaded as CSV and imported from CSV or Excel (`ContactImporter`). On the audit trail as `contact.created`, `contact.updated` and `contact.deleted`.
- Filed tax returns (`FiledTaxReturn`): Mark as filed keeps a return's figures for its months, shown later beside what the books give now, box by box; returns of a form can't overlap unless one is withdrawn with a reason. On the audit trail as `tax_return.filed` and `tax_return.withdrawn`.
- Customer and supplier statements (`PartyStatement`, the Statements page, linked from the aging reports): the opening balance, the invoices, bills, payments, credit notes, refunds and reversals in a run of days with the running balance, and the closing balance with its aging; or the open items at the end. They print on their own for sending and download as CSV or Excel; All customers (or suppliers) prints every statement with something to send, one to a page. `AgingReportGenerator` takes `party:` to age one party's items.
- Budget vs actual on the profit and loss (Compare with a budget, `Reports::BudgetComparison`): actual, budget, variance and percentage for the period, and for a month the year to date too, each variance marked favourable or unfavourable; in the CSV and Excel downloads as well. The demo has a budget for this year.
- A tax code that recurring entries or bank rules use can't be deleted (it was refused by the database, not explained).
- Bank statements import from OFX and QFX files (OFX 1.x SGML and 2.x XML, bank and credit card) and ISO 20022 CAMT.053 statements as well as CSV, told apart by their contents, with the file's closing balance on its last line. `BankStatementImporter.call` now also returns the `format` it read.
- Recurring entries: templates of lines with a schedule (every N weeks, months or years, on a day or the last day, until a date or a number of entries), making drafts or posted entries, optionally reversed the next month. `RecurringEntryRunner` (and `RecurringEntriesJob`, for the host to schedule) makes the entries due, catching up, once each; problems are kept on the template and shown. A Recurring entries page, Make recurring on an entry, and audit events.
- `Entry.build_from_ruby_hash`: the entry `create_from_ruby_hash` would create, unsaved.
- Auto-reversing entries: an entry with `reverse_on` (the form's "Reverse automatically on", or `create_from_ruby_hash(reverse_on:)`) posts its reversal on that date when it is posted, in the same transaction. Refused for invoices, bills, payments and credits, and when no financial year covers the date.
- `USAGE.md`: setting up an organization's books step by step, in the pages or from the console and rake tasks. Its examples run in the suite.
- The dashboard warns, and `tudla_accounting:balances:check` prints a warning (without failing), when an organization with accounts has none marked retained earnings, receivable or payable while `carrying_amount_sources` records that side, or unrealized or realized exchange gains once one of its accounts is held in another currency: without them nothing is tracked or carried, and nothing else says so.
- `cash_account_codes` can be a callable given the organization; `TudlaAccounting.configuration.cash_account_codes_for(organization)` reads it.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-06

Tagged but never published. Later versions don't support upgrading from it (or from 0.1.0): until a release is published, start from a fresh install.

### Added
- A tax return template for the Philippines' quarterly VAT return, BIR Form 2550Q (April 2024): `TaxReturnForm.from_template!(organization, "ph_2550q")` lays out Part IV's computation (items 31A to 61B) and Part II's total payable (items 15 to 26) with their totals, for mapping the VATable, zero-rated and exempt sales and the purchases to tax codes
- Indirect-method cash flow statement (`Reports::CashFlow.new(..., method: :indirect)`, or the page's Method choice): net profit plus each balance-sheet account's change, by activity, matching the direct method's totals.
- Entries can be created from a foreign-currency bank account's statement lines, converted at a rate given or the rate provider's rate for the day, with the statement amount as the bank line's foreign amount.
- Cash-basis tax summary: tax on invoices, bills and taxed credit notes counts as they are settled (each allocation's share, reversed if taken off); entries with no receivable or payable count when posted. Choose it on the page, per call (`basis: :cash`) or with the `tax_basis` setting.
- Lines with a foreign amount can be taxed: the tax is worked out on the converted amount and posted in the organization's currency (an inclusive line's foreign amount is split too). A tax account held in another currency is refused.

### Fixed
- The amount fields of the invoice, entry, payment, opening balance, budget and bank rule forms showed amounts with the currency's own decimal mark (`1000,00` in euros), which they then read as a hundred times as much: saving a budget or bank rule unchanged multiplied its amounts by 100. Every amount field is now filled the way the forms read it (`tc_amount_field`).
- An invoice line's quantity typed with a thousands separator (`1,000`) was refused as not a number, though the form's total read it; it is read as amounts are, and one that isn't a number says so by its line.
- Bank statement CSVs: an amount written with a decimal comma (`1.234,56`) was read as 1.23, a `CR` or `DR` marker was ignored, and a minus sign after a currency symbol (`$(3.00)`) was lost. The importer now takes the file's `decimal_mark` (asked for on the banking page next to the date order), reads signs, currency symbols and codes, and refuses an amount it can't read for certain instead of guessing.
- A weekly recurring entry fell on the wrong days: the first came six days after its start, and one changed part-way through a week could skip a week.
- Clearing a line's tax code on a draft invoice or bill didn't take it off.
- `Entry.create_from_ruby_hash` stored a foreign amount given as a decrease (`EUR -55.00`) as a negative number; it is now a magnitude, like the line's amount.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-06

First release.

### Books
- Chart of accounts as a tree, with contra accounts, foreign-currency accounts and a cash flow activity per account; load it from CSV or Excel with opening balances, or enter opening balances on a form.
- Financial years split into months (calendar or fiscal), closed and reopened in order with a reason; nothing posts into a closed period.
- Journal entries as drafts, posted into the month of their date and reversed (never edited) once posted; `Entry.create_from_ruby_hash` with idempotency keys, and entries from host-app records.
- Balances kept per account and period with automatic year-end carry-forward to retained earnings; a balance check and rebuild from the posted entries.

### Receivables and payables
- Invoices, bills, payments, disbursements, credit notes, supplier credits and refunds by configurable source class; credits applied to invoices and bills through allocations (one payment across many invoices, oldest first, taken off again), with aging reports.
- Foreign currency: realized exchange differences on settlement, period-end revaluation with automatic reversal, and exchange rates from a configurable provider (Reserve Bank of Australia included).

### Tax, banking and dimensions
- Tax codes for sales and purchases, taxed lines (tax added or split out) from the API and the entry form, and a tax summary.
- Bank statement CSV import, suggested and manual matching, entries for bank-only lines, and a reconciliation summary.
- Reporting dimensions (department, project...) tagged on entry lines, with profit and loss by dimension.

### Reports
- Balance sheet, profit and loss, trial balance, general ledger (with CSV), cash flow (direct method), aging, tax summary and profit and loss by dimension.

### Integrity and integration
- Unique keys and checks in the database; on PostgreSQL, triggers that refuse changes to posted entries, posting into closed periods and edits to the audit trail (reinstalled whenever a schema is loaded).
- An audit trail of everything done to the books, with an activity page.
- Hooks for host apps: `current_organization`, `current_actor`, `authorize` (read/record/post/administer), `TudlaAccounting.subscribe` events after commit, and `rails generate tudla_accounting:install`.
- Web pages for all of the above, styled with Tailwind CSS v4 and following the host's light/dark theme.
- Set-based posting and batched balance reads for large ledgers; `perf/benchmark.rb` to measure them.
